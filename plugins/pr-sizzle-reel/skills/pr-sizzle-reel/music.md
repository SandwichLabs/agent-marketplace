# The song: picking, mapping, rights

Phil picks the song from his own music library. Your job is to pick well with him, find the moments the edit hangs on,
and make sure he can use it.

## Picking a track (if he hasn't chosen)

Ask him for two or three candidates and go through these with him:

1. **A real drop.** A moment where the song hits noticeably harder after a build. The whole reel is built around it.
   No drop, no sizzle: a steady track works for a calm class recap, not a hype reel.
2. **Tempo about 118–130 BPM.** Fast enough for a cut on every beat, slow enough to read the text. A half-time feel
   (about 60–65 BPM) can work if it's counted in doubles.
3. **The drop comes early enough.** For a 30 s reel he needs about 15 s of build before the drop; for 60 s, about 40 s.
   You can start the reel partway into the song.
4. **Lyrics stay out of the way.** An instrumental or a clean version is easiest. Sung words fight on-screen words;
   explicit lyrics are out for a studio account.
5. **A clean ending.** A hit, a stop or a bar you can cut on. Not a long fade.

## Mapping it

Ask Phil for these, or work them out together:

- **BPM.** Many libraries list it. If not: tap along for 15 seconds, count the taps, and multiply by 4. Round to the
  nearest whole number.
- **The drop**, to the second ("1:12"). Have him play the build and say "now" when it hits.
- **The lift**: what happens just before the drop (a riser, a stop, a drum fill, a shouted line). The on-screen shout
  ("ONE · MORE · REP, · Y'ALL!") lands there.
- **The stretch to use:**
  - 30 s reel: start about 15.5 s (8 bars at 124 BPM) before the drop.
  - 60 s reel: start about 41 s (21 bars) before the drop.
  - Then end on a bar line, ideally on a hit.

  Work the seconds out from his BPM (bar = 4 × 60 ÷ BPM) and tell him the exact in and out points.

If code execution is available and he uploads the audio file, you can confirm the tempo and the drop from the file
(energy jumps at the drop). His ears are the final say: if he says the drop is at 1:12, it's at 1:12.

## Rights (check before posting)

Instagram mutes or blocks reels with music the account isn't allowed to use. Ask where the track came from:

- **A royalty-free or subscription library** (the kind made for creators): check that his licence covers social posts
  for a business, and keep the licence or receipt. Some libraries ask for a credit in the caption.
- **A commercial song** (something on the radio): don't put it into the CapCut export. Export the reel with no music
  and add the song inside Instagram from its own music picker. Then align it by ear to the drop. Business accounts
  only get Instagram's commercial-use library, so the song may not be there.
- **Not sure?** Treat it as commercial.
